New campaigns begin in the bootcamp. Existing valid Opening Shift field saves resume their field job; use the new **Bootcamp** button to open training without losing that work. An existing job is not relabelled as completed training. The older 0.6 clinic remains separate at `dist/`.

Start with the free **Equipment & cable guide** if students need a hardware review. Then work through the six lessons. Each of the first five uses a short explanation, an actual mentor demonstration and a fresh learner task. The final troubleshooting challenge uses a different fault and requires the learner's own test, relevant repair and explanation. Students may rewatch demonstrations and retry practice; all training support is free.

## What students learn and do

| Lesson | Foundation review | Practical task |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meet the equipment | Workstation, service host, switch, power and network interface; device role versus equipment appearance | Select and power the bench hardware |
| Build a wired path | Ethernet patch cable and compatible ports; a complete path versus a loose cable or powered device | Connect workstation and host through different free switch ports, then test |
| IP address and subnet mask | IPv4 interface address, unique host, local network, mask and `/24` | Read the network card, apply an unused address with the supplied mask and retest |
| Add wireless access | AP versus router; power, Ethernet uplink, Wi-Fi association and coverage | Make the mobile device's path work and test it in both rooms |
| Choose access | Network reachability versus authorization; staff workflow and outsider restriction | Choose a responsible policy and test allowed and deliberately denied requests |
| Diagnose a changed fault | Inspect, test, identify the affected layer, change a relevant cause, retest | Investigate a valid-address setup with a disconnected uplink; repair and explain why an address edit could not replace that connection |

The material guide also distinguishes power, USB and display cables from Ethernet. These are conceptual comparisons; the interactive connection tool models Ethernet and Wi-Fi. Routers, gateways, DHCP and Internet routing are introduced as boundaries or later topics, not falsely claimed as configured here.

## How the address lesson teaches understanding

The mentor uses `192.168.60.0/24`; the learner uses `192.168.80.0/24`; the final changed setup uses `192.168.90.0/24`. The independent clinic still uses `10.40.10.0/24`. Students need to read the current card rather than copy a full demonstrated address.

The supplied mask is `255.255.255.0`. `/24` means 24 network bits. Comparing the first three decimal groups is a consequence of this particular mask, not a general rule for all subnets. A host needs an unused usable address; a valid-looking string can still be a duplicate or outside the relevant subnet. For these `/24` benches, `.0` is the network address and `.255` is broadcast.

Keep the configuration task separate from a service result: a broader valid mask can still leave both peers on-link and pass this particular service test. The lesson still asks for the supplied `/24` configuration. The model reports that task mismatch separately rather than inventing a network failure. An address or mask change cannot supply a missing physical cable.

## Controls and help

Choose devices and ports with named buttons and dropdowns; precision dragging is unnecessary. Read the mentor's current step, use the inspector for the chosen device, apply settings, and run a service test. The diagram shows actual setup and the selected recorded test. A line does not independently prove that service is available.

Training observations are labelled as mentor demonstration or learner practice. A mentor pass cannot complete the learner's attempt. After a causal change, retest; an old observation is historical evidence. Wireless practice requires actual tests in both rooms. The checklist explains what is still needed, and the model decides whether the lesson can be completed.

Open the how-to/material guide or graduated hints whenever needed. There is no training cost, real countdown or speed score. Client-job help retains its separate fictional tariff; returning to training preserves the field setup and does not solve it. Training equipment carries no contractor earnings.

## Completion and teacher evidence

Once all six practical tasks are recorded, a new campaign can enter the first client job. The clinic starts with its own briefing, equipment and evidence requirements. Training is preparation, not independent field completion.

The final training explanation is retained exactly, or marked for oral discussion. Presence of an answer permits the activity record; the game does not judge the quality of the explanation by keywords. Hints, speed, income and completion do not award an IB grade or establish mastery. The teacher can use observation, conversation and the exported product together.

Progress export contains the restorable training and field campaign. Readable evidence gives per-lesson task summaries, provenance, the transfer explanation and field evidence. Detailed JSON keeps the recorded actions and observations. No student name, patient information or account is requested; keep identifying information out of free text.

## Saving and recovery

Work remains in the same browser profile and site origin. Before moving between home and school, download **progress**, then import it on the other device. Evidence is for review and is not a substitute for a progress backup. School profile cleanup, private browsing or storage/download restrictions may remove or prevent local retention.

Opening training and returning to field work preserves the field campaign and the field editor's separate draft scope. Retry starts a fresh training attempt and does not award another client payment. Read-only tabs must explicitly take control before changing progress. A rejected damaged import must leave current work untouched.

## Walkthrough and pilot

Allow a separate preparation session initially. A provisional planning allowance is about 35–45 minutes for the six training lessons (the lesson plan allocates 42 minutes) and 20–30 minutes for field work, with flexibility to stop and resume. These are estimates, not measured student timings or limits.

First, walk through a new campaign yourself, then resume an existing field save through the Bootcamp button. Check whether a beginner can identify the correct item/port, explain the mask, apply rather than merely type a setting, read a diagnostic cause and choose an appropriate repair.

Then observe three to five beginners on actual school computers. Note where they hesitate, whether the material guide helps, whether they can explain the changed fault, and whether help remains comfortable to use. Check downloads, reload, two tabs, keyboard access, zoom and assistive technology. Stop for false success, loss of work or an inaccessible required control.
