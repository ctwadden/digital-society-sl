## What the first mission teaches

Students choose equipment for four jobs, connect a local network, investigate an inherited address error, test permissions and explain their decisions. The Computers lesson supplies Riverbend’s fixed reception, mobile nursing, week of home monitoring and central-storage needs. The network fault, access policies, return-and-sync boundary and fictional business costs are additions for this simulation.

The game uses a real shared state: a device, cable, address, permission or repair must affect the tests. The interface may display a result only when the engine produces it. This first clinic is one local subnet; no router, Internet connection, DHCP service or DNS lookup is needed. Wearable collection is offline and is transferred after return. No patient records, health readings, student names or real accounts are requested.

The ten I-can statements are student-friendly learning targets. Their `cc.clinic.*` identifiers are internal design references, not official IB outcome or Evidence Map codes. Full alignment and limits are documented in `SOURCE_ALIGNMENT.md`.

## Suggested lesson sequence

1. **Teach one visible connection.** Start with Jo's prepared setup and two goals. Let the learner select ports, connect, test, deliberately unplug, observe, repair/retest and explain. Trainer-supplied equipment is identified; this lesson is separate from independent equipment choices.
2. **Use free independent bootcamp.** Introduce the four clinic jobs as their decisions become relevant. Each station offers explanation, a modelled example, the student's own practice and a changed example. The same engine and controls are used in the client mission.
3. **Begin the client practical.** Students make their own decisions. The how-to guide is available for an explicitly quoted fictional fee and simulated consultation time.
4. **Collect the handover.** Students export actual results and their own reasoning. An oral explanation can be arranged, with its pending status recorded honestly.
5. **Discuss a changed situation.** Ask a short transfer question and review the evidence that supports the student’s explanation.

Bootcamp and first-mission timings have not been measured with students. Allow a separate orientation if the class is new to addressing or the interface. There is no real-time countdown or penalty for slow reading.

### Two introductory learning goals

- I can connect available network ports and test whether a service is reachable.
- I can explain why a connected cable and a working service are different claims.

Ask the learner which parts carried the request, why a new test was necessary after changing a cable, and what the result does and does not establish. Observe use of controls, discuss the explanation and inspect the export as distinct evidence opportunities. The teacher still decides whether understanding has been demonstrated.

Open **Teacher-selected clinic practice** and choose **Start free clinic practice** when students need the complete clinic task with free guidance and no earnings. This uses the same network rules, faults, access decisions and evidence requirements as the client. It creates a separate attempt and does not award bootcamp completion or a grade. Any existing client job, guide charges and payment are preserved; **Return to saved client job** resumes that job.

This is a local classroom choice, visible to students. It is not authenticated teacher-only access. It avoids accounts or a new PIN system. Decide with the class when to use it. Practice cannot pay a contract; return to the saved client before settling an eligible delivered job.

Opening help is appropriate learning behavior. The ordinary controls, diagnostics, glossary and accessibility guidance remain free in every mode. Monitor whether the fictional client tariff discourages help and use free practice when needed. There is no wall-clock countdown.

## Expected clinic walkthrough

This is an expected path for teacher/QA review, not a record of a test already completed. Use the actual device and port labels shown by the build.

### Equipment route A

- Reception: desktop.
- Nurse: tablet with a separately attached sensor dock.
- Home monitor: wearable.
- Central host: small server, with services explicitly enabled.
- Network: 4-port switch and access point.

Power the devices that need to operate. Connect reception, the server and the access point to three different switch ports. Associate the nurse’s Wi-Fi with the access point. The fourth switch port is spare. The access point covers exam rooms A and B in the model; the home inset is outside coverage.

### Equipment route B

Use a laptop at reception with an explicitly attached Ethernet adapter and a laptop for the nurse with a sensor dock. The central host may remain the small server; a suitable general-purpose computer configured in the server role is also permitted by the catalog. Check every required capability and actual port. Do not assume that a laptop picture supplies Ethernet, or that an Ethernet adapter is a sensor dock.

The teacher should ask why the second route is suitable and what it gives up. The simulation accepts capabilities and valid paths; it does not automatically judge a nuanced trade-off explanation.

### Diagnose the nurse’s inherited setting

The clinic’s network is `10.40.10.0/24`, mask `255.255.255.0`. The server is `10.40.10.10`, reception is `10.40.10.20`, and the nurse initially inherits `10.40.20.30`.

Once the physical/wireless path works, the nurse’s service test should fail for the wrong network range. Choose an unused permitted nurse address, such as `10.40.10.30` or `10.40.10.31`, then repeat the workspace test. Exact host values are examples; the model should accept another valid unused host address. A duplicate active address or the subnet’s network/broadcast address should fail appropriately.

An early correct repair is permitted in the client. Its evidence must say no pre-repair test was run if that is what happened. Do not make a student break a valid network merely to create a failure record. The initial profile is seeded once; moving/reloading/replacing a device must not fabricate a fresh scripted fault or erase its provenance.

The final suite checks both room contexts from one snapshot. These engine checks are distinguishable from actual player movement tests. They should not claim the student personally visited a room when they did not.

### Complete the wearable journey

1. Run the collection check at home to produce seven nonclinical day markers.
2. Return the wearable to the clinic.
3. Attach a compatible sensor dock to the nurse companion.
4. Hand off the markers to that companion.
5. Upload them through its working network path and the submit-only transfer role.
6. Test the current intake path and permissions.

These are separate domain steps. Home collection does not imply clinic connectivity. A successful historical receipt remains a fact if the network later breaks, but handover still needs a current valid path. Re-uploading the same marker IDs must not duplicate data or earn money. The check does not establish medical accuracy, a battery duration or live remote monitoring.

### Test two responsible access choices

| Role | Separate responsibilities | Nurse also books |
|---|---|---|
| Reception | Bookings | Bookings |
| Nurse | Workspace | Workspace and bookings |
| Marker transfer | Submit markers | Submit markers |
| Outsider | No staff service | No staff service |

Both choices can satisfy the proposed clinic brief. Compare a clear division of tasks with flexibility for follow-up bookings. Reception must not gain the workspace merely because nurses can book. Marker submission must not provide workspace browsing.

Explore broad access and deny-all, then return to an appropriate policy. Broad access exposes staff functions; deny-all prevents necessary work. The expected denial must arise from permissions on a healthy reachable service. Unplugging the server cannot prove privacy protection.

If the policy changes after the student wrote the privacy explanation, revisit that explanation. The app should flag the context change rather than silently treating an older rationale as reasoning about the new policy.

## Guide costs and the business record

The user-requested mechanic is a paid technical consultation during an active client contract. Current pilot balancing values are provisional:

| Item | Credits | Simulated time |
|---|---:|---:|
| One new guide section | 5 | 3 additional consultation minutes |
| All three sections, maximum per contract | 15 | 9 additional consultation minutes |
| All hint tiers inside a purchased section; later reopening | 0 extra | 0 extra |
| Bootcamp, ordinary diagnostics, glossary, controls and accessibility | 0 | 0 |
| Guide review after delivery, even before payment settles | 0 | 0 |

The quote appears before acceptance. Cancelling the quote costs nothing. No real money is involved, and actual reading time is never billed. There is no expiry that prevents completing the work. The pilot reports **additional simulated consultation minutes**; it does not invent total elapsed working time or calculate a timeliness bonus.

The gross service fee is 120 fictional credits. Guide costs are reserved against that fee rather than deducted from current cash. A student with zero cash can still obtain help. Two sections reserve 10 credits; the eventual net payment is 110. The wallet distinguishes earned fees, settled expenses and cash balance, while active work shows reserved costs and estimated net payment.

Successful handover freezes its technical/evidence snapshot and the complete fee/guide cost basis together. Settlement uses that frozen basis once. Exports, retries, repeated delivery clicks, marker uploads and reopening messages must not pay again. Later guide review cannot add deductions to a delivered contract.

Business results are motivating game feedback. Credits retained, clients served and support purchases do not automatically establish competence or change an IB grade. Support use is context for discussion, not proof that a learner is incapable.

## Handover and teacher review

The four prompts ask for equipment reasoning, repair evidence, access consequences and one limitation. They contain no prewritten student rationale. Free-text fields remind learners to avoid identifying information. The engine verifies presence or a recorded oral arrangement, not the quality of the explanation.

If written responses exist, export those words. If an oral explanation is arranged, the report must say the conversation remains pending; it cannot claim an explanation was supplied or reviewed. A partial export should remain available before the job is ready.

Use the exported product alongside observation and conversation:

- **Observation:** watch the student identify a test’s failed stage, choose a relevant change and retest.
- **Conversation:** ask why that change helped, why another change would not, and what would differ in a new situation.
- **Product:** inspect equipment, actual configuration, current/history results, repair provenance, effective access rules and the student’s explanation.

A useful review pattern is application after bootcamp, a materially different problem, and a changed-situation explanation. Three opportunities prompt review; they are not an automatic mastery threshold. Record only what the evidence supports. Canonical course/skill/outcome IDs and any future Evidence Map adapter require separate reconciliation; this pilot makes no automatic record or grade writes.

The new bootcamp captures the first prompt below in its own attempt. Its failure, reconnection, retest and exact explanation (or pending oral status) remain in campaign practice evidence after students move on. Possible follow-up prompts:

- The access-point uplink is unplugged. Would changing the nurse’s address repair that problem? Explain and demonstrate.
- The clinic adds a second network in another wing. Which boundary is outside the present one-subnet model?
- A nurse now needs to arrange bookings. Which permission could change while reception and outsider restrictions remain?
- Home information must arrive live instead of after return. Which assumption in this pilot no longer fits?

## Save, reload, reset and export

Progress is local to the device, browser profile and exact site origin. A different browser, school profile cleanup, private session, cleared site data or changed preview URL may lose or hide the save. It is not a cloud backup. A shared browser profile may expose the previous person’s local work; provide an end-of-session clear action after export.

Use **Download progress** for a restorable campaign file. Use **Export evidence** for readable/JSON teacher evidence; an evidence file is not interchangeable with a progress file. Import should validate before replacing a current run. Local files can be edited and are not tamper-proof assessment submissions.

If the device's file picker is unavailable, open **Progress → Paste backup JSON** and paste the complete contents of your downloaded progress file. Choose **Review pasted backup**, then confirm the import. The same size limit, save validator and ownership rules apply; damaged content must leave current work unchanged. The text stays in the browser and is not uploaded. Cancel clears the pasted text; a rejected import retains it for inspection. This alternative does not establish that the native file picker works on your school's computers.

Unapplied address-form changes and explanations awaiting a successful save can be rescued with **Download unsaved draft**. That file is labelled unsubmitted and is not simulation evidence. It is never automatically merged into imported/newer progress. Apply changes, confirm the saved status, or rescue the draft before closing/reloading; unapplied forms are not automatically restored after a hard reload.

The visible saved indicator must reflect a successful write. If browser storage is blocked or full, the game should remain usable in memory and offer export with a clear unsaved warning. Test the school’s download/import policy rather than assuming it permits files.

Reset boundaries:

- **Active undelivered client:** a new client attempt under the same contract, retaining already granted guide sections and reserved costs.
- **Delivered or settled contract:** a separate free practice clone, preserving the original client attempt, locked delivery and payment.
- **Full campaign clear:** export-first/cancel options, then clear this game’s wallet/history together; do not clear unrelated site storage.

Multiple tabs must not silently overwrite or double-settle a run. The release test record should identify how the implemented persistence layer handles that case.

## Small student pilot and remaining decisions

Follow the four-to-six-student protocol in `qa/SCHOOL_PILOT.md` before a full class. Record aggregate findings in the teacher’s existing workflow; the app adds no tracking. Review whether the phone helps the task, the guide tariff discourages necessary support, and explanations match actual actions.

Teacher review decisions:

- Does return-and-sync meet the intended home-monitoring scenario, or is live delivery needed for a later redesigned mission?
- Do the two nurse-booking policies fit the intended discussion?
- Are the bootcamp explanation depth and the 5-credit/3-minute consultation tariff appropriate for this class?
- How will the short handover supplement the existing full writing task and teacher evidence record?

School browser behaviour, managed-profile retention, download permissions, assistive-technology use and student engagement remain unverified until actually tested. Do not infer classroom readiness from successful loading or unit tests alone.

## Optional Digital Society discussion

Open **Content connections** alongside the I-can statements when you want a debrief. Ask learners to use their actual configuration and tests:

- **Systems:** Which technical part and human procedure must both work? How would one failure affect a person?
- **Power:** Who gains the ability to act when nurses can make bookings? Who should authorize or question that access?
- **Values and ethics:** Which needs compete? Give a benefit, a barrier and a condition that would change the recommendation.

Health provides the client context; economics/media extensions stay deferred. These are source-grounded concept lenses, not invented outcome codes or additional completion gates. See SOURCE_ALIGNMENT.md for the supplied textbook pages.

## New recovery details in revision 0.4

Unapplied address entries and unsaved explanation drafts are kept separate from recorded simulation state. **Download unsaved draft** creates a plainly labelled rescue text file, not a completed action or teacher evidence. A read-only tab can export its recorded snapshot and rescue its drafts. Taking control loads the latest saved campaign; preserve a draft before discarding or replacing it. Rejected imports and cancelled reset choices leave recorded work intact.

The clinic picture separates the remote home site from the clinic boundary. Solid cables and dashed radio associations describe physical/configured connections. The latest-test panel states which observation is current, failed or historical and traces only the path recorded by that observation. Read the diagnostic; a connection line alone is not a passed service test.
