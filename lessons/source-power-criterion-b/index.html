0–5 minutes

Set up your evidence document.

Create a Google Doc titled IBDS · Source Power + Criterion B · Your Name, or continue in the document your class normally uses for IA research.

Before opening the sources

  1. Write one sentence defining power in a digital system.
  2. Name one human part and one technical part that might interact in a facial-recognition system.
  3. Name two defensible values that could pull a decision about facial recognition in different directions.

Keep this first attempt visible. Do not search for definitions yet.

5–23 minutes

Use two sources with different jobs.

Captions, the TED transcript and text-to-speech are allowed. Record exact timestamps or paragraph details so another person can trace your evidence.

Source A · 8:27 video

Joy Buolamwini: How to protect your rights in the age of AI

Watch for claims about biometric systems, unequal consequences, human rights and who has the power to demand change.

Watch the TED talk ↗

TEDAI · October 2023. Speaker perspective: computer scientist, artist and algorithmic-justice advocate.

Source B · official technical summary

NIST: Demographic effects in face recognition software

Read the opening findings and the explanation of false-positive and false-negative errors. Notice what NIST tested and what it did not claim to explain.

Read the NIST summary ↗

National Institute of Standards and Technology · 19 December 2019. Technical evidence remains useful, but its date and tested systems must be stated.

Do not ask which source is “better” in general. Ask what each source can credibly contribute to a specific inquiry.
23–38 minutes

Compare claims, evidence and perspective.

Copy the prompts below into your document and answer in concise notes.

  1. What is one important claim from Buolamwini? What example or evidence supports it? Include a timestamp.
  2. What is one important NIST finding? What testing or evidence gives it authority?
  3. How do the sources relate: agreement, qualification, tension or complementary focus? Explain the relationship.
  4. Power: who can design, purchase, deploy, regulate or challenge these systems?
  5. Systems: how are data, the model, the organization and a later human decision connected?
  6. Values & Ethics: what two defensible values or rights are in tension?
  7. What remains unresolved after using both sources?
Quality check: A source relationship is more than two summaries. Use one specific detail from each source and explain why their relationship changes or limits a conclusion.
38–67 minutes

Begin Criterion B with your Source #1.

Open your existing source shortlist. Choose the strongest candidate for your current inquiry question. Today you are building structured evidence notes, not writing a polished Criterion B paragraph.

Source #1 audit

  1. The source: record full title, author or organization, date, URL or DOI and source type.
  2. Citation: write it in the same style you will use in your reference list.
  3. Claims and perspectives: identify two important claims, findings or pieces of evidence. State whose perspective is represented and whose is absent.
  4. Credibility: explain what expertise, method, evidence, organization or publication process supports the source. Address currentness where it matters.
  5. Justification for use: explain exactly what part of your inquiry question the source helps investigate and how you expect to use it in later analysis.
  6. Boundary: state one conclusion this source cannot establish by itself.

Source-set decision

Finish with: What kind of second source would corroborate, qualify or challenge Source #1? Which stakeholder or perspective is still missing?

If you do not have your shortlist with you

Use one candidate source you previously saved. Do not spend the class collecting random links. Complete the audit and mark it KEEP, MAYBE or REPLACE. If no candidate source is available, use the NIST source as practice and clearly label it COMMON PRACTICE — NOT YET MY IA SOURCE.

67–70 minutes

Finish with a traceable decision.

  • □ My common-source comparison uses details from both sources.
  • □ My Source #1 notes have five clearly labelled parts.
  • □ I explained usefulness to my exact inquiry rather than calling the source “reliable.”
  • □ I identified a missing perspective or next source type.
  • □ I recorded timestamps, paragraphs or page details that another person can check.
Submit or share the document through the class’s normal submission channel before leaving. If that channel is unavailable, keep the document in your school Drive and be ready to show its revision history next class.

Captions, transcripts and accessibility tools are permitted. Do not use generative AI to write the source analysis or Criterion B notes. The evidence, judgment and wording must be yours.
