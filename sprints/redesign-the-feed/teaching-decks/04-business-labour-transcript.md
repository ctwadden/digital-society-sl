# Digital business and labour: incentives, opportunities and power

4.2A and working-practice parts of 4.2B · pp. 213–219

## 1. Digital systems change how organizations work

The economic context examines how people work and exchange goods and services. A business uses digital systems to coordinate employees, record transactions and understand demand. A platform can also decide which seller or creator a customer encounters. We will connect a system’s mechanism to opportunities and constraints for different people. A successful business outcome does not automatically mean every worker or customer benefits. This deck covers the assigned business and working-practice sections, and identifies the remainder of the economic chapter.

## 2. Operation and organization of a business

Consider a school event shop. Each sale updates a transaction record. Summing sales by item can inform the next stock order. A shared calendar helps volunteers coordinate, and a shared document records decisions. The system connects information to action. The textbook describes movement towards more dynamic teams, but that is a possible organizational change rather than a universal outcome of using software. A company can adopt digital tools while retaining a strict hierarchy. Explain the specific change in the example you use.

## 3. Customer value and business incentives

A subscription service may value renewal, a marketplace may earn a commission on completed sales, and an advertising-funded service may gain from opportunities to show ads. These are possible incentives, not proof of the objective used by every business. A platform’s ranking choice can support one of those outcomes. Distinguish a claim about the business model from evidence about an actual ranking rule. Consider customer value, operating costs and other constraints as well as revenue.

## 5. Diversification and workforce diversity

The chapter uses diversity in two related but different senses. A business may diversify what it sells, and it may seek a diverse workforce. These should not be confused. An inclusive organization also examines whether people can participate effectively, rather than simply counting demographic categories. Digital recruitment can broaden access, but an unsuitable screening rule can exclude applicants. ## 10. Working practices and office design

The textbook describes start-ups, office design and digital working practices. A start-up is an organization in an early stage of development. Funding and product uncertainty can affect workers. Flexible spaces and shared tools can support coordination, yet open rooms can also distract people and some tasks need privacy. The historical descriptions of particular company offices are not current evidence of employee experience. Judge the arrangement against the task and the people who use it, including access, concentration and boundaries between work and personal time.

## 11. Remote work and digital nomadism

An employee working from home can be remote without being a digital nomad. A person travelling while completing online work may be both. The system requires suitable devices, networks, access to files and ways to coordinate. That can expand location choices, but time zones, unreliable access and data handling can introduce costs. Permission to work across borders depends on the specific setting, so a course discussion should investigate the relevant rules rather than assume freedom to work anywhere.

## 12. Crowdsourcing and crowdfunding

A group asking people to submit designs is crowdsourcing. A group asking for donations to produce an exhibition is crowdfunding. One gathers contributions of work or information, and the other gathers money. A crowdsourced collection may contain errors or uneven perspectives, so someone still needs to evaluate it. Crowdfunding does not guarantee a project will succeed. Use a specific example and identify what is being contributed. No account creation, payment or participation in a labour platform is required for this lesson.

## 13. Microwork, gig work and sharing

These categories can overlap, but they emphasize different features. Labelling individual images for payment is microwork. A platform matching a customer with someone to complete a delivery illustrates gig work. Renting access to equipment through a booking service illustrates a sharing arrangement. A creator earning advertising revenue is not automatically performing microwork. Explain the task, payment arrangement and platform’s role before drawing comparisons. Avoid assuming the same working conditions across all digital work.

## 14. Platforms can mediate access to work

A matching platform reduces the effort needed to find a customer. It may also set the conditions under which a worker appears, receives a task or challenges a rating. That changes the power relationship. A numerical rating can affect visibility without revealing the context of a complaint. These are mechanisms to investigate in a specific case rather than universal findings about every platform. Trace the information that enters the allocation and identify who can change a record or contest the result.

## 15. Employee organizations and bargaining power

The textbook explains employee organizations and collective bargaining, and raises the contrast between employment and contracted task provision. Digital coordination can help workers share information and organize. The applicable rights and benefits depend on law and the actual relationship in a particular jurisdiction. We will not label every gig worker the same way. An inquiry needs a defined place, period and source. The conceptual question is how the platform’s control and the worker’s ability to negotiate affect the distribution of benefits and risks.

## 18. An impact becomes an implication through a reasoned link

Consider our invented museum feed. A discovery weight increases exposure for an unfamiliar topic. A possible immediate impact is that a new creator gains opportunities to be seen. A possible implication is a broader range of creators sustaining an audience, but that requires continued exposure and suitable income opportunities. The implication is not simply a more dramatic synonym for the impact. State the time horizon, the affected group and the conditions linking them.
