Real cases: TikTok, YouTube and platform labour

Your commission: prepare a defensible briefing for a school digital-wellbeing group and student media team. They want to know whether a feed can improve discovery while respecting users and supporting creators. Choose a priority; support it with a mechanism and evidence. This classroom commission is invented. The TikTok investigation and YouTube policies below are real.

Case boundary: recommendation, user control and creator opportunity

TikTok describes a personalized recommendation system. In February 2026, the European Commission reported a preliminary finding concerning addictive design under the Digital Services Act, including a highly personalized recommender alongside other features. YouTube publishes policies for eligible creator revenue sharing. These establish different things: a provider account of a mechanism, a regulator’s preliminary risk finding and a payment contract. They do not establish an individual creator’s income or the causal effect of one design feature.

Inquiry: To what extent should a feed prioritize engagement rather than discovery, and what can the available evidence tell us about the consequences?

SOURCE A · 18 June 2020

How TikTok recommends videos #ForYou

TikTok Newsroom · Read the primary source

Assigned locator: Sections “What factors contribute to For You?” and “Diversifying recommendations”; locate the paragraph beginning “A strong indicator of interest…”

Paraphrased claim: The account groups signals into interactions, video information and device/account settings. It describes finishing a longer video as a stronger indicator than shared country, and acknowledges filter-bubble risk.

Origin, purpose and evidence: Official provider explanation of design; intended to explain and build trust. It is a primary account of what the provider states, not an independent measurement.

Relationship to another source: Compare its risk acknowledgement with C. Different dates and questions mean one cannot simply “prove” or “disprove” the other.

Limit: Historical description; exact current weights, individual experiences and independently measured harm are absent.

SOURCE C · 6 February 2026

Commission preliminarily finds TikTok’s addictive design in breach of the Digital Services Act

European Commission · Read the primary source

Assigned locator: Opening finding and sections on addictive design/risk assessment; paragraph listing infinite scroll, autoplay, push notifications and the highly personalized recommender.

Paraphrased claim: The Commission reports a preliminary finding concerning addictive design. Its investigation considers scientific research, internal documents/data and expert interviews, among other evidence.

Origin, purpose and evidence: Official EU regulatory communication summarizing an investigation. It has a regulatory purpose and provides a bounded account, rather than raw study data for classroom re-analysis.

Relationship to another source: Relate its personalization concern to A’s description of signals and diversification. Separate acknowledged risks from demonstrated safeguards.

Limit: Preliminary, not final. Several design features are considered together. It does not isolate the recommender’s causal effect or establish worldwide creator income.

SOURCE Y · Undated; checked 4 October 2026

YouTube partner earnings overview

YouTube Help · Read the primary source

Assigned locator: “How much does YouTube pay?” → ad revenue sharing: Watch Page Monetization Module and Shorts Monetization Module.

Paraphrased claim: The policy distinguishes 55% of net Watch Page ad revenue from 45% of a Shorts Creator Pool share allocated to the creator. Other earning routes include fan funding.

Origin, purpose and evidence: Platform policy/help page: useful for contractual bases and eligibility language; not a dataset on typical earnings.

Relationship to another source: Connect revenue eligibility and allocation to the exposure mechanism. More exposure alone does not prove paid or higher-valued views.

Limit: No guaranteed payment; different percentage bases; no individual earnings or distribution data.

SOURCE L · 23 February 2021

Rapid growth of digital economy calls for coherent policy response

International Labour Organization · Read the primary source

Assigned locator: Opening summary and paragraphs describing digital labour platforms, workers and policy responses.

Paraphrased claim: The research summary situates platform work within a global digital economy and discusses opportunities and working-condition challenges.

Origin, purpose and evidence: ILO research communication based on its platform-work report; a broader labour lens than a provider’s product description.

Relationship to another source: Use with A/Y to ask who performs work and who controls market access. It adds context, not TikTok-specific wage evidence.

Limit: Historical, cross-platform summary. Do not treat it as a 2026 survey of TikTok creators or infer current wages.
