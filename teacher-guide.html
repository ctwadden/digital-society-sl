Enter the Inquiry

Digital Society analysis begins where a technical mechanism meets human institutions, unequal stakeholder effects and contestable choices about what should happen next.

Pacing decision: five foundation classes feed a three-class assessed sprint. The sprint can be compressed to two classes only by combining testing, revision and submission with the second production class.

Your ten-minute pre-read

The expert frame

The public evidence supports a cautious chain; it does not reveal every technical step. Mark unknowns rather than inventing certainty.

The collections remaining secure did not mean services could simply restart. Data integrity and operational availability are different system properties.

An impact is a direct effect. An implication is longer-term significance and should state timeframe, probability or a limiting condition.

A concept earns its place only when it changes what the case helps us explain. Systems reveals interdependence; power reveals control; values reveals priorities.

A Grade-7-oriented judgement weighs evidence, stakeholder difference and uncertainty, then answers with degree and conditions rather than absolute language.

Questions students may ask

Was missing MFA the whole cause?No. The ICO linked missing MFA to escalation, but a responsible explanation also considers privilege, architecture, legacy coupling, recovery capability and the limits of public evidence.
If the collections were safe, why was the impact serious?A collection can remain intact while the systems needed to discover, request and use it are unavailable. Access is part of the institution's social function.
What makes this evaluation rather than description?The response explains mechanism, compares stakeholder effects, tests a counter-perspective and makes a conditional judgement supported by attributed evidence.
Your through-line: Return every answer to mechanism → visible or sourced evidence → consequence. Students should hear you explain why a decision works, not only where a control is located.
Assignment map

What the product actually requires

1. Class A · Establish the case

Separate fact from inference, build the supported attack-and-recovery mechanism and complete the three-source matrix.

2. Class B · Analyse significance

Map two distinct stakeholders, apply the strongest 3C lenses and distinguish immediate impacts from conditional implications.

3. Class C · Evaluate and judge

Write and calibrate one Paper 1-style PEEL + Evaluate paragraph containing a counter-perspective, trade-off and qualified judgement.

Assessment boundary: F-P-DS-U01-001 | British Library Source-Lens Dossier is recommended as formative or revision-eligible. Confirm achievement by inspecting the required evidence; the website checklist and quiz never assign mastery.

Day 1

The catalogue goes dark

Say this to launch:
“Today’s puzzle is What does a digital failure reveal about society? Before we name tools or concepts, commit to one observation and the evidence that made you notice it.”
0–8 · HOOK

Begin with an unavailable-catalogue screen and a table of researchers, staff, publishers and the public. Predict what each person can no longer do.

8–20 · EXPERT EXPLANATION

A digital society inquiry connects a technical system with people, institutions and consequences. The British Library case makes this connection visible because the physical collection survived while systems for discovering and requesting knowledge failed.

20–35 · MODEL

Demonstrate the first three actions. After each one, point to the visible result and explain why the choice matters.

35–60 · GUIDED WORK

Release students into the remaining steps. Pause once for a partner evidence check.

60–67 · CHECKPOINT

One fact, one inference and one unanswered question are clearly separated; the system map contains technical and human components.

67–70 · EXIT

Complete: This case belongs in Digital Society because...

Question: What does a digital failure reveal about society?
Key language: Digital society, digital systems, impacts, implications, inquiry, claims, evidence and the 3Cs.
Mechanism: A digital society inquiry connects a technical system with people, institutions and consequences. The British Library case makes this connection visible because the physical collection survived while systems for discovering and requesting knowledge failed.
Evidence due: One fact, one inference and one unanswered question are clearly separated; the system map contains technical and human components.

Evidence due: Two stakeholders have distinct impact chains; at least one implication includes a timeframe and condition; the counter-perspective uses case evidence.

35–60 · GUIDED WORK
