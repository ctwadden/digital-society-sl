Enter the Inquiry

Digital Society analysis begins where a technical mechanism meets human institutions, unequal stakeholder effects and contestable choices about what should happen next.

Pacing decision: three full 70-minute classes. Each class completes named Operation 7 gates and preserves evidence in one unified dossier.

Your ten-minute pre-read

The expert frame

The public evidence supports a cautious chain; it does not reveal every technical step. Mark unknowns rather than inventing certainty.

The collections remaining secure did not mean services could simply restart. Data integrity and operational availability are different system properties.

An impact is a direct effect. An implication is longer-term significance and should state timeframe, probability or a limiting condition.

A concept earns its place only when it changes what the case helps us explain. Systems reveals interdependence; power reveals control; values reveals priorities.

A Grade-7-oriented judgement weighs evidence, stakeholder difference and uncertainty, then answers with degree and conditions rather than absolute language.

Questions students may ask

Was missing MFA the whole cause?No. The ICO linked missing MFA to escalation, but a responsible explanation also considers privilege, architecture, legacy coupling, recovery capability and the limits of public evidence.
If the collections were safe, why was the impact serious?A collection can remain intact while the systems needed to discover, request and use it are unavailable. Access is part of the institution's social function.
What makes this evaluation rather than description?The response explains mechanism, compares stakeholder effects, tests a counter-perspective and makes a conditional judgement supported by attributed evidence.
Your through-line: Return every answer to mechanism → visible or sourced evidence → consequence. Students should hear you explain why a decision works, not only where a control is located.
Assignment map

What the product actually requires

1. Class A · Gates 1–3: establish what can be claimed

Define the technology/system, reconstruct the supported incident-and-recovery mechanism, mark unknowns and complete the British Library/ICO/NCSC evidence matrix.

2. Class B · Gates 4–6: analyse significance

Differentiate stakeholders, select the strongest 3Cs, build impact-to-implication chains, then add a source-grounded counter-perspective and trade-off.

3. Class C · Gate 7: judge and calibrate

Write the response from the dossier, compare it with weak/secure/Grade-7-oriented exemplars, revise the weakest gate and defend the final degree judgement.

Pathways that add real complexity

Tier 1 · Structured dossier

Use the seven labelled frames and sentence stems; every Operation 7 gate must still contain case evidence.

Tier 2 · Independent synthesis

Choose and organize evidence independently while maintaining all seven gates and explicit source limitations.

Tier 3 · Transfer challenge

Apply Operation 7 to a second digital-society case and compare how context changes the judgement.

Operation 7 completion gates

1Technology and the precise digital system
2How it works: supported causal mechanism
3Attributed evidence with origin, usefulness and limitation
4Distinct stakeholders plus the strongest 3C lenses
5Immediate impact and conditional implication
6Counter-perspective and genuine trade-off
7Qualified judgement: extent, conditions and uncertainty

Day 1

Operation 7 Gates 1–3: claim only what evidence supports

Say this to launch:
“Today’s puzzle is What happened, how did it work and what can the sources actually establish? Before we name tools or concepts, commit to one observation and the evidence that made you notice it.”
0–8 · HOOK

Open with the catalogue-going-dark scenario. Students distinguish what they observe, what they infer and what remains unknown.

8–20 · EXPERT EXPLANATION

Responsible analysis separates the technical chain from the evidence chain. Every causal arrow needs a source, an inference label or an explicit unknown marker.

20–35 · MODEL

Demonstrate the first three actions. After each one, point to the visible result and explain why the choice matters.

35–60 · GUIDED WORK

Release students into the remaining steps. Pause once for a partner evidence check.

60–67 · CHECKPOINT

Gates 1–3 are complete: precise system definition, supported/inferred/unknown mechanism map and three-source matrix with limitations.

67–70 · EXIT

Which arrow in your mechanism is strongest, which is uncertain and why?

Board plan

Question: What happened, how did it work and what can the sources actually establish?
Key language: Technology/system definition, access and privilege, exfiltration, service loss, recovery, fact versus inference, source origin/purpose/usefulness/limitation and triangulation.
Mechanism: Responsible analysis separates the technical chain from the evidence chain. Every causal arrow needs a source, an inference label or an explicit unknown marker.
Evidence due: Gates 1–3 are complete: precise system definition, supported/inferred/unknown mechanism map and three-source matrix with limitations.

Day 2

Operation 7 Gates 4–6: analyse significance

Say this to launch:
“Today’s puzzle is For whom did the system failure matter, through which lens and with what trade-off? Before we name tools or concepts, commit to one observation and the evidence that made you notice it.”
0–8 · HOOK

Assign the same supported fact to four stakeholders and compare how the consequence changes.

8–20 · EXPERT EXPLANATION

The same technical event produces unequal consequences because stakeholders occupy different positions in the system. Evaluation tests the first claim against evidence that qualifies it and choices that sacrifice one value for another.

20–35 · MODEL
