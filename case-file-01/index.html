Stakeholders: understand the idea, then identify them yourself

What is a stakeholder?
A stakeholder is a person, group, organization, or institution that is affected by a digital system/decision or can influence what happens.

Example unrelated to this case: In a food-delivery app, a delivery driver is a stakeholder because the platform can affect the jobs they receive, their ratings, and their income.
YOUR TASK
Identify at least THREE specific stakeholders in the Clearview / facial-recognition case.
FOR EACH ONE
Explain: How are they affected? What do they want, gain, risk, or control?

POWER

Power helps you investigate who can influence or control others, how power is embedded in organizations/governments, and whether power is distributed unequally.

  • Who can decide?
  • Who can collect or identify?
  • Who can refuse?
  • Who can challenge an error?
  • Who can regulate whom?

Your Paper 1-style 8-mark question

To what extent should Canadian law-enforcement agencies be permitted to use facial-recognition systems built from images collected from the public internet?

Do your research and planning in the Google Doc template. Your final response must answer the exact question and follow what the command term requires.

How to structure your one-page response

Aim for about one page of focused writing. This is not a strict word count; roughly 350–450 words is a useful target if typed. Your answer should read like a short argument — not a list of worksheet responses.

PARAGRAPH 1 · POSITION + CONTEXT
  • Answer the question immediately.
  • Name the Clearview / Canadian law-enforcement context.
  • Identify the key tension.
  • End with a provisional or conditional thesis.
PARAGRAPH 2 · STRONGEST ARGUMENT
  • Make one clear claim.
  • Use specific case/research evidence.
  • Connect it to a stakeholder.
  • Use Power or Values & Ethics to deepen the analysis.
  • Explain why the evidence matters.
PARAGRAPH 3 · COUNTER-ARGUMENT + WEIGHING
  • Give the strongest reasonable opposing argument.
  • Anchor it in a stakeholder perspective.
  • Support it with evidence.
  • Compare the competing concerns.
  • Decide which should carry more weight and why.
PARAGRAPH 4 · JUDGEMENT
  • Return directly to “To what extent.”
  • Make a clear supported judgement.
  • State conditions, safeguards, limits, or circumstances.
  • Do not simply repeat your introduction.
USEFUL CHECK: CLAIM → EVIDENCE → EXPLANATION → STAKEHOLDER / CONCEPT → WEIGH → LINK BACK TO THE QUESTION
